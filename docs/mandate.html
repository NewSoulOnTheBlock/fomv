01 · The gap

fomo already copies trades. It does not pool them.

fomo's copy trading is per-account: your wallet, your balance, your strategy slot. There are no shares, no NAV, no fee split, and no way for a trader to run size on behalf of people who believe in them.

Everything that makes pooling hard follows from one fact: a vault's equity moves independently of the leader's. Deposits arrive, withdrawals leave, and the two books diverge from the first trade onward. Every design decision below is downstream of that.

02 · Sizing

Mirror the weight, not the notional

A copy bot mirrors dollars: the leader spent $4,000, so you spend $4,000 times some multiplier. That number is meaningless to a pool whose equity changed twice this morning. A vault mirrors the fraction of the book the leader actually committed.

leader committed 4% of their book → vault commits 4% of vault equity

This is scale-invariant. It survives deposits landing mid-position, it survives the leader adding or withdrawing capital, and it means a $2k depositor and a $2m depositor get the same strategy rather than the same dollar amounts.

Entries and exits need opposite rules

This asymmetry is load-bearing, and collapsing it into one rule is the classic copy-bot bug. If a position has 10×'d since entry, portfolio-weight logic computes an exit size unrelated to what the vault actually holds.

Sizing basis by direction
ActionSized fromWhy
Entry Leader's portfolio weight Conviction is a fraction of their book, never a dollar amount
Exit Leader's position fraction “Sold 60% of their position” maps onto our holding at any value

03 · The clamp stack

Every mirror is sized, then survived

A raw mirror size passes through each constraint in order, and the engine records which one actually bound. Below is a real ladder from a mainnet dry-run: a $20m vault following a leader with a $54m book who put 0.03% of it into one token.

Recording the binding constraint matters more than it looks. It is what lets a depositor read why the vault took a smaller position than the leader did, rather than inferring that the system is broken.

04 · Guardrails

Bounds on risk-taking only

One rule governs the whole set: guardrails restrict risk-taking, never risk-reduction. Nothing that blocks a buy blocks a sell — including the deny list, because deny-listing an asset must not trap depositors inside it.

05 · Execution

The speed problem, answered honestly

The core execution guardrail

You cannot promise a vault will beat the leader's fill. Depositors are seconds behind by construction. What you can promise is a bound on the cost of being slow.

If the price has already run past the leader's fill by more than maxLeaderPriceDriftBps — 3% by default — the vault skips the trade entirely. No position beats buying their exit liquidity.

Execution limits
LimitDefaultBounds
maxLeaderPriceDriftBps300How far past the leader's fill we will still enter
maxTradeAgeMs20,000Chasing a signal that has gone cold
maxSlippageBps150Applied to price impact as well as quote deviation
reentryCooldownSec60Fee churn from a leader flip-flopping

Detection reads balance deltas, not DEX logs

fomo routes through aggregators, so one tap in the app can touch Raydium, Meteora, Orca and a pump.fun curve in a single transaction, each emitting a different event shape. Net balance deltas are venue-agnostic and collapse multi-hop routes for free:

USDC → SOL → BONK nets to (−USDC, +BONK)

The intermediate SOL leg can never be mirrored as a trade of its own. Decoding per-leg events and mirroring each is how a copy bot ends up buying the router's intermediate token and immediately re-selling it.

06 · Accounting

What makes it a vault and not a bot

Shares, NAV, and a fee split. All of it on 1e18 fixed-point integers, because a 1e‑16 float error repeated across thousands of deposits mints shares out of nothing, and every existing depositor pays for it.

Anti-dilution levy

A deposit into a fully-invested vault forces the vault to go buy the whole book at market. Without a levy, the incoming member gets shares at a clean NAV while existing members silently eat the slippage. The levy prices that cost, is paid by whoever causes it, and stays in the vault for the members who bore it. It scales with how invested the vault actually is — depositing into a vault sitting in USDC forces no trading and costs nothing.

Per-lot high-water marks

Each deposit is its own lot with its own mark, consumed FIFO. A single share-weighted average mark lets a member top up after a drawdown to drag their own mark down, then pay fees on gains that merely recover earlier losses. Lots defeat that.

Fees crystallise as share transfers

Not as cash. Nothing leaves the vault, no position has to be liquidated to pay a fee, and NAV per share does not jump on the fee event. The leader simply ends up owning more of the same pool.

07 · Open

What is not built

The mirror engine, guardrails and ledger are working and tested against Solana mainnet. These four are the remaining gaps, roughly in order of how much they gate a launch.

01

Custody

The engine currently assumes a keypair it controls. A real product needs either a program-owned vault with on-chain share accounting, or a regulated custodial arrangement. This is the decision that gates everything else.

02

Deposit and withdrawal plumbing

The ledger computes shares, levies and fees correctly. Nothing yet wires it to actual transfers.

03

Durable state

The cursor and the processed-trade set live in memory. They must survive a restart or the vault re-mirrors trades it already took, with real money.

04

The other chains

fomo also trades Base, BNB, Ethereum, Monad and Robinhood Chain. The chain adapter is the seam; the mirror and ledger layers are already chain-agnostic.

Copy-trading replicates another account's transactions at the operator's sole direction. Nothing here is investment advice. Operating a pooled vehicle that takes other people's money is a regulated activity in most jurisdictions — take advice before accepting a single outside deposit.
